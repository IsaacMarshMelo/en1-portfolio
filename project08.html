Project 8: Astronaut Tools

This project challenged us to think like human factors engineers and design a one-handed LEGO Education SPIKE Prime tool for an astronaut on a spacewalk (EVA). The tool had to move nuts from one bolt to another. This was a hypothetical classroom challenge; astronauts are not using LEGO Education SPIKE Prime kits in space.

Design requirements

The tool had to unscrew a nut from one bolt, screw it onto another bolt, and hold onto the nut the entire time it was off a bolt.

It also had to stay in the user's hand or be tethered, work with either hand while the other hand remained free, and be usable with bulky gloves and limited dexterity. The task was intended to take seconds rather than minutes.

Evaluation

A successful run started with both nuts on one bolt and ended with both nuts on the other. Each run had to be completed one-handed while wearing ski gloves. Runs were tested with the right hand, the left hand, the board upright, upside down, and on its side. The rule was simple: drop a nut and it floats away, ending the run.

In a class head-to-head bracket, two students raced side by side and the fastest successful run advanced. Using a second hand or dropping a nut or tool meant losing the round.

My design and reflection

My tool used a pressure sensor to control the speed of a motor that turned my screwing and unscrewing mechanism. The design was compact compared with many others in the class and had average speed. I had a lot of fun with the project and made it to the semifinals. Consistency, stability, and reliability helped my tool advance, even though it was not the fastest.

If I approached the project again, I would try a gear ratio to increase the screwing speed. I would also consider layering rubber bands across the screwdriver arms to create a larger gripping surface for the nuts, an idea inspired by a classmate's design.
